Commercial Roof Condition: Evidence to Collect

A repeatable record helps owners, facility teams, contractors, and consultants discuss the same roof conditions. Arrange safe access and use inspection methods appropriate to the roof assembly.

ObservationRecordQuestions for follow-up
Membrane blistering, open seams, or delaminationLocation, extent, close photographs with scale, membrane type if known, and whether the area changes after weather.Is the condition isolated or widespread? What do the surrounding layers and seams show?
Cracking or surface wear on built-up / modified-bitumen roofingPhoto and roof-plan location; note whether reinforcement or underlying layers appear exposed.Does the finding require a closer assessment or a core sample? Is the deck condition known?
Standing water or drainage concernDrain and overflow locations, approximate ponding boundaries, photos, and time observed after rainfall.Could blocked drains, low areas, insulation, slope, or deck conditions contribute? Check applicable warranty documents.
Suspected wet insulation or interior moistureInterior leak locations, dates, weather, thermal images if taken, and any test method or report.What method was used, what did it measure, and where should findings be confirmed by targeted investigation?

Thermal images, moisture surveys, electronic leak detection, and core samples have different purposes and limits. A test result should be interpreted with its method, conditions, roof assembly, and inspection scope documented.

Decision framework

Compare the Scope Before Comparing the Total

Repair

Ask which defect is being addressed, what nearby materials were inspected, and whether recurring leaks suggest a wider issue. Record any temporary measure separately from permanent work.

Restoration or coating

Ask whether the existing roof is suitable, how moisture and damaged areas will be evaluated, what repairs and preparation are included, and which product and warranty conditions apply.

Replacement

Compare removal, disposal, substrate and insulation assumptions, drainage, flashing, edges, penetrations, access, occupied-space protection, and how concealed conditions are handled.

Operating and financial assumptions

Separate quoted project costs from estimates for future repairs, disruption, energy, financing, and tax. Identify who supplied each assumption and how uncertain it is.

Facility handover checklist

Build a Useful Roof Record

  1. Mark the roof areas on a plan and assign consistent location names.
  2. Record roof type, approximate age, known repairs, leak dates, and available warranty documents.
  3. Store dated photographs with the observer, location, weather context, and any test method.
  4. List drains, overflows, rooftop equipment, access points, and sensitive areas below.
  5. For each proposal, note included areas, preparation, materials, exclusions, schedule, cleanup, and approval steps for extra work.
  6. After work, retain closeout photos, invoices, product information, warranty documents, and maintenance requirements.

How should I compare two commercial roof estimates?

Compare the same roof area and system, then list tear-off/recovery, disposal, insulation, cover board, flashing, drainage, deck repairs, access, permits, schedule, and warranty requirements. Record exclusions and allowances beside each total.
