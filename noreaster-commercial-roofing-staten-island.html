What should you do if your roof leaks during a nor’easter?

Keep people away from the affected area, protect belongings only where it is safe, and call a roofing contractor to discuss the leak. Do not climb onto the roof during strong winds or heavy rain. If water is near electrical equipment, a ceiling is sagging, or a tree has struck the building, keep clear and seek emergency assistance when there is immediate danger.

Why a nor’easter can expose roofing problems

A nor’easter is an East Coast storm named for the northeasterly winds commonly experienced along the coast. Rain, strong winds and coastal flooding can occur together; snow is not required. The National Weather Service’s nor’easter guide explains the storm pattern.

For a roof, the concern is the combination of repeated rain and wind acting on vulnerable details. A loose flashing edge, an aging repair or a damaged seam may become noticeable only when wind drives rain against it. Debris can obstruct drainage or strike the roof surface. A roof that stayed dry in a brief shower can still leak during a longer storm.

There is no single gust speed that guarantees damage to every roof. Installation, condition, building shape, exposure and the roofing system all matter. A forecast alone cannot establish whether your roof needs replacement.

Commercial roofing: flat-roof details to inspect after the storm

On stores, offices, warehouses and other commercial properties, a leak can interrupt operations as well as damage finishes. Maximum Roofing’s commercial roofing services include evaluating leaks and discussing repair, maintenance and replacement options. The inspection should follow the evidence rather than assume the entire roof has failed.

Membranes, seams and perimeter edges

TPO, EPDM and modified-bitumen roofs have different materials and repair methods. Inspection may identify punctures, separated seams, loose edge details or failed transitions. A repair material suitable for one system may not be suitable for another. Covering a wet or damaged area with a generic coating is not automatically a lasting solution.

Drains, scuppers and rooftop equipment

Leaves and storm debris can interfere with drainage. Flashing around equipment curbs, pipes and roof-to-wall transitions also deserves attention. Ask the contractor to explain the likely water-entry point and contributing conditions. The place water appears indoors may not be directly beneath its source.

Interior damage and roof condition

Record where and when leaks occur, whether they change with rainfall, and any earlier repair history. Inspection findings may support a localized flat roof repair, further investigation or a broader replacement assessment. Hidden moisture and damaged decking need to be considered when defining the work.

Safe steps while you wait for roofing assistance

  • Keep people away from wet electrical equipment, unstable ceilings and storm-damaged sections of the building.
  • Move stock, furniture or valuables only if the area is safe to enter. Contain a small drip only where doing so creates no electrical or structural risk.
  • Take photos from a safe indoor or ground-level position. Note when the leak began and which rooms are affected.
  • Do not climb ladders, clear roof drains or attempt a tarp installation during dangerous conditions.
  • Keep away from fallen wires and flooded roads. Follow emergency instructions and report immediate threats to emergency services.

Coastal flooding and a roof leak are different problems. Roofing work cannot prevent tidal water from entering a low-lying property. Follow NWS flood-safety guidance and evacuation directions where applicable.
