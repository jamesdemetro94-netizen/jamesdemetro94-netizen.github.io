How to read this. The common horizon is selected as 5, 10, 15, or 25 years. Undiscounted totals add annual costs and subtract visitor-entered remaining-life/deferral credits at the horizon. NPV discounts each year’s net cash flow at the editable rate. Only annual repair estimates escalate by their relevant editable rate; disruption cost stays flat and baseline expected event cost is probability × event cost per year. Project costs occur at year 0 and renew at the entered service-life interval; restoration renewals use the optional renewal rate, or the initial rate when left blank. Insurance savings reduce costs only when supported by written estimates; energy savings are visitor inputs and grow only by the editable energy-price assumption. Exposure value and moisture percentage never enter the cost formulas.

Energy savings are entered separately for replacement and restoration; both default to $0 and should be supported by a building-specific estimate. Taxes, financing, and accounting treatment are not modeled. Consult your tax adviser. Assumptions appendix

—
Formulas:
  • Year-t repair cost = entered repair estimate × (1 + relevant escalation rate)t−1. Disruption costs remain days × cost/day without repair escalation; only the post-project repair estimate escalates at its entered rate.
  • Expected baseline major-event cost = entered event probability × entered event cost per year; this estimate is not escalated.
  • Annual net cost = repair + disruption + expected event cost − written insurance savings − energy savings.
  • NPV = year-0 project cost + Σ(year-t net cash flow ÷ (1 + discount rate)t) − present value of entered residual credit.
  • Cost per service year = project cost ÷ expected service life + average annual net operating cost over that life; baseline uses average annual operating cost over the selected horizon.
  • Cumulative payback = first time accumulated baseline-minus-restoration cash flows recover restoration capital and modeled renewals. Discounted payback discounts each year's difference.
  • Sensitivity varies nonzero eligible assumptions one at a time or repair and project-price inputs by ±30%; see the selected comparison and chart labels. The range is not probabilistic.

Transparent method

Transparent method and formulas

Version 1.1 · Updated October 20, 2026. The calculations compare three cash-flow scenarios using the same selected 5-, 10-, 15-, or 25-year horizon. Nonzero advanced assumptions are editable placeholders, not claimed industry averages.

Expand formulas and assumptions

Repair-as-needed baseline

Repairs in year t = annual repair estimate × (1 + baseline escalation)t−1. Disruption = days × cost/day. Repairs escalate at the baseline rate; the annual expected event cost is probability × event cost and remains flat. Baseline annual total is their sum.

Replacement and restoration

Year 0 project cost = roof area × entered cost per sq ft. Each later year's net cost = post-project repairs + disruption − written insurance savings − energy savings. Post-project repair estimates escalate at the entered post-project rate; disruption costs remain flat; energy savings escalate at the entered energy inflation. If a stated service life ends before the horizon, a renewal cost is charged; restoration uses the optional renewal rate when entered and otherwise reuses the initial rate.

Undiscounted total and NPV

Undiscounted total = sum of annual net costs and project costs − residual/deferral value entered at the horizon. NPV = year-0 cost + sum of year-t net cash flow ÷ (1 + discount rate)t − discounted terminal residual value.

Annualized service-year cost

For replacement or restoration: initial project cost ÷ entered service life + average annual net operating cost over that service life. For the repair baseline: average annual baseline operating cost over the selected comparison horizon. These are planning comparisons, not a service-life guarantee.

Payback and IRR

Cumulative restoration payback is the first point when accumulated baseline-minus-restoration cash flows recover restoration’s upfront and any modeled renewal cost. Discounted payback does the same after discounting each year's difference. IRR uses baseline-relative cash flows and is omitted when the flow pattern has no single meaningful rate.

Low / base / high sensitivity

The low and high scenarios reduce or increase annual repair estimates, baseline expected event cost, and project rates by 30%. Disruption, energy and insurance savings, discount rate, service life, and residual values stay unchanged. The range is a scenario comparison, not a probability interval.

Disruption cost remains optional and user-entered; no sector-average revenue-loss factor is inserted. The optional “inspection-informed” label only reflects that age and moisture fields have values; these values are not independently verified and do not confirm an inspection occurred.

FAQ

Calculator questions

Does the calculator create a roofing quote?

No. It models scenarios from values entered by the visitor and does not inspect the roof or create a contractor proposal.

Are escalation, failure probability, or savings based on Maximum Roofing project records?

No. The calculator uses no Maximum Roofing project dataset or sourced failure-rate benchmark. The displayed 10% baseline escalation, 3% post-project escalation, 6% discount rate, 25-/10-year service lives, 15% event probability, $25,000 event cost, and 3% energy inflation are editable illustrative placeholders only—not measured rates, industry averages, or forecasts. Insurance and energy savings start at $0.

Are energy or insurance savings guaranteed?

No. They are optional assumptions. Enter them only when supported by project-specific estimates or written information from the relevant professional, utility, insurer, or broker.

Does the calculator determine restoration suitability or roof service life?

No. Financial scenarios do not establish roof condition, restoration suitability, code compliance, or expected life. Use roof-specific inspection findings, written proposals, and qualified professional review.

Can I share a scenario?

Yes. The share URL encodes inputs. Do not include confidential or sensitive information.
